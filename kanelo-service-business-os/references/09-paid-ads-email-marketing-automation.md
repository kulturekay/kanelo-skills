# Paid Ads, Email Marketing & Automation

> **Client Acquisition · File 5.**
> Files 1–4 cover the *channels* that generate leads (content, funnels, hot & cold outreach). This file covers the **paid engine and the messaging that powers it** — how to run paid ads that acquire clients predictably, the copywriting frameworks that make any ad/email/page convert, and how email sequences + automation catch and nurture the leads paid traffic produces.
>
> Two threads run through it: **(1) copy & creative are the new targeting** — since iOS14, messaging is what wins; and **(2) match the message to the prospect's stage of awareness** — what you say changes entirely depending on how much the prospect already knows.

---

## 1. Why messaging is everything now

The "set it and forget it" marketer died after iOS14. **Copy and creative are the new targeting.** The platforms can no longer micro-target the way they used to, so the message itself does the targeting work — the right words find the right person.

The stakes, in order:
- If you can't **hook** a prospect with your ad → you won't get the click.
- If you can't clearly **communicate your value** → you'll lose the lead.
- If you can't **sell your offer** → you'll lose the client.

Marketers who pour their resources into nailing messaging have longevity; the rest don't. Everything below is about applying best-practice messaging *inside an ad account.*

## 2. How the Meta algorithm works (the core mental model)

Spend level determines traffic temperature:

> **The more you spend, the colder the traffic you'll be served — and the better your ads & offer need to be.**

- Low spend → the algorithm serves your **hottest** traffic first (people most likely to convert).
- As you scale spend → it reaches **warm**, then **cold** traffic.
- Cold traffic is less primed, so your **ad + offer have to be stronger** to convert it.

Implication: scaling spend isn't free growth — it's a test of whether your messaging and offer hold up as audiences get colder. Strong copy/offer = you can scale; weak copy/offer = you hit a wall the moment you push past your warm pool.

## 3. Ad-account structure — two ways to build it

You have two options when structuring the account. Pick the variable you want to *test* at the ad-set level.

### Option A — Target by Persona (Persona Testing, ABO)
- **Structure:** Campaign → **Persona** ad sets → 3 ads each.
- **What you're testing:** *targeting* (which persona responds) at the ad-set level.
- **Budget:** **ABO** (Ad-set Budget Optimisation) — budget set per ad set, so each persona gets a fair, controlled test.
- **Use when:** you're still learning *who* your best audience is.

```
PERSONA TESTING (ABO)
 ├─ Persona 1 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 ├─ Persona 2 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 ├─ Persona 3 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 └─ Persona 4 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
```

### Option B — Scale by Angle (Angle Scaling, CBO)
- **Structure:** Campaign → **Angle** ad sets → 3 ads each.
- **What you're testing:** *angle* (which message/hook wins) at the ad-set level.
- **Budget:** **CBO** (Campaign Budget Optimisation) — budget pooled at campaign level, so Meta pushes spend to the winning angles.
- **Use when:** you know your audience and want to scale the best-performing messages.

```
ANGLE SCALING (CBO)
 ├─ Angle 1 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 ├─ Angle 2 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 ├─ Angle 3 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
 └─ Angle 4 → Ad 1 / Ad 2 / Ad 3
```

> **The progression:** start with **Persona/ABO** to discover who and what works in a controlled test, then graduate winners into **Angle/CBO** to scale spend behind the messages that convert. ABO = discovery, CBO = scale.

## 4. The ad-optimisation framework (operating cadence)

Run the account on a daily / weekly / monthly rhythm:

| Cadence | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Check performance. Increase/decrease spend — **scale up ~20%/day when results are there.** |
| **Weekly** | Implement new action points (new ads, new audiences, new bidding strategies). Optimise: **turn off losers, double down on winners.** |
| **Monthly** | Full funnel-performance review. Introduce new lead magnets and funnels. |

> The daily layer manages money against current results; the weekly layer manages creative/audience iteration; the monthly layer manages the funnel itself. **20% daily is the safe scaling step** — push harder and you destabilise the algorithm's learning.

## 5. Top-of-funnel & lead magnets

**90% of your marketing should be top-of-funnel prospecting.** And **the quality of your lead magnets heavily determines success** — the magnet is the thing cold traffic actually opts in for, so it carries the campaign.

### The three lead-magnet types

| Lead magnet | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Webinar** | Creates a pool of **high purchase-intent** leads; establishes credibility/authority; delivers strong value | Setting up **strategy calls / high-ticket offers** |
| **Free training** | Delivered as videos or written resources; typically a **longer email chain** lead magnet | An **extended window to convert** leads over a sequence |
| **Instant resource** | Workflows, checklists, processes repurposed into a downloadable | Attracting **mass leads** cheaply — **lowest cost per lead** |

> Choose by intent vs volume: **webinar** = fewest but hottest leads (→ high-ticket calls); **instant resource** = most but coolest leads (→ cheap top-of-funnel volume); **free training** = the middle, and the one that runs on an **email chain** — the bridge into the email/automation layer below.

## 6. Stages of Awareness — the master messaging principle

What you say depends entirely on **what the prospect already knows.** Before creating any ad (or email, or page), decide which stage your prospect is at, because the message changes completely.

| Stage | Who they are | How you speak to them |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem | **Stories, education** |
| **Problem Aware** | Understand the problem, but have no solution | **Actively call out the pain:** *"if you're [this], then you need this"* |
| **Solution Aware** | Know solutions exist, but not *yours* | **Compare to known competitors, call out benefits, show use cases** |
| **Most Aware** | Know the problem *and* that your product exists | **Offers, UGC, testimonials, proof it works** |

Example of why it matters: pushing someone into a 1-1 discovery call after a lead-magnet opt-in requires *very* different copy for a problem-aware prospect vs. a solution-aware one. Map the stage first, then write.

> **Worked application:** brands like Obvi, Hims, and Loop Earplugs run *different ads for each awareness stage* (Unaware → Problem Aware → Product Aware → Most Aware), moving the same prospect down the ladder with stage-appropriate messaging rather than firing one message at everyone.

## 7. Gamify copywriting — the four frameworks

You only ever need four copy frameworks. Match the framework to the format.

| Framework | Stands for | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **PAS** | Pain, Agitate, Solution | Long-form copy: sales pages **with price**, plain-text emails, webinar structure |
| **BAB** | Before, After, Bridge | **Testimonials & case studies** (use when asking for client reviews) |
| **AIDA** | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | **Short copy**: promoting offers, short-form lead-capture pages |
| **4 Ps** | Problem, Promise, Proof, Proposal | Long-form, unique offers (*"we'll get you [x] in 90 days or work free until we do"*) — similar to PAS |

### Two high-converting fill-in templates

**Framework #1 — BAB (before → after → bridge):**
> *"[X] years ago I was [painful before]. Since then I have [core achievements / social proof]. I did it by [process]. No one is talking about this — why? Because there's [lack of knowledge/guidance/support]. [Debunk belief 1]. [Debunk belief 2]. [Debunk belief 3]. I've put together a piece that shows you exactly how to [end result from the hook]. All you have to do is [CTA]. [Risk reversal / scarcity — it's free, there's a guarantee, the offer's closing soon.]"*

**Framework #2 — AIDA (attention → interest → desire → action):**
> *"99% of [niche] get [X] WRONG. You can't [outcome] by [commonly-held belief]. BUT you can [desired outcome] by [your solution]. In fact, the [process/method] I've used to [outcome] has helped [social proof] to… [list 3 outcomes]. I've put together a piece that shows you exactly how to [end result]. All you have to do is [CTA]. [Risk reversal / scarcity.]"*

Both end the same way: **value asset → CTA → risk reversal/scarcity** — the same value-first arc as the cold-email sequences in File 4.

## 8. The Ad Grid — systematising creative production

The Ad Grid is how you generate many ads systematically instead of guessing. It's a matrix: **Personas across the top, Angles down the side**, and you write a hook for every cell.

### Structure (worked example for an agency-services advertiser)

| Angle ↓ / Persona → | New business owner (1–2 yrs) | Established e-com brand | Agency owner seeking clients |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Case study / testimonial** | *"In [x] time we took [y] to [z]"* | *"ROAS doubled since working with [x]"* | *"We'd hit a growth ceiling for [time]. [x] helped us overcome it"* |
| **Uncertainty** | *"Avoid the common mistakes [x] make with [y]"* | *"Stubborn CPA blocking growth? Drop CAC by adopting [x]"* | *"Get ahead of 99% of peers with [x] tactic"* |
| **Frustration** | *"Struggling to keep more than 2–3 clients on the books?"* | *"You need THIS if your agency has a content bottleneck"* | *"Want to go beyond referrals? Try THIS outreach technique"* |

Each cell = a ready ad hook aimed at one persona through one emotional angle. Filling the grid gives you a full creative testing matrix (which then feeds the Persona/ABO or Angle/CBO account structures in §3).

### Research that feeds the grid
Before writing, answer these about the ICP (so the hooks are real, not guessed):
- Where do they get their information?
- What type of content/creative do they like?
- What problems do they face?
- What has this ICP likely tried before?
- What roadblocks does this prospect face?

> This research step is the same "specificity converts" discipline as the IACP work — the grid only produces good ads if the persona/angle cells are grounded in real ICP knowledge.

## 9. Email Marketing & Automation

Paid ads and lead magnets generate opt-ins; **email + automation is what converts them over time.** This layer connects directly to the funnel automation in *Landing Pages, Funnels & VSLs* (File 2).

- **The free-training lead magnet *is* an email play** — it's "a longer email chain lead magnet" giving "an extended window to convert leads." The multi-day training sequence (Day 1 → Day 6, each delivered on a landing/sales page) in File 2 is exactly this mechanism.
- **Automation triggers** (File 2): squeeze page → autoresponder fires → drip sequence; VSL page → Zapier → auto email flow on "showed up"; non-converters → Pixel → retargeting ads. Email and ads work together — retargeting keeps the cold/un-converted warm while the email sequence nurtures the opted-in.
- **Copy frameworks apply to email too** — **PAS** is explicitly called out for plain-text emails; **BAB/AIDA/4Ps** structure nurture and offer emails. The **stages-of-awareness** model decides each email's job: educate the unaware, agitate the problem-aware, differentiate for the solution-aware, present offers/proof to the most-aware.
- **Newsletters** (from *Inbound Leads*, File 1) are the organic-side email engine: weekly-to-everyone (also on LinkedIn) and monthly-to-clients — the owned-audience complement to paid lead-magnet sequences.

> **The combined engine:** paid ads (cold→warm→hot, scaled via ABO→CBO) drive opt-ins through a quality lead magnet → email automation nurtures them with awareness-matched, framework-driven copy → retargeting ads catch everyone who didn't convert → warm leads route to a call. Ads buy the attention; email and automation extract the value from it over time.

## Synthesis — how this fits the acquisition system

This file is the **paid + messaging core** that powers the other channels:

1. **Paid ads** are a fifth acquisition engine alongside inbound, funnels, hot and cold outreach — but uniquely, they let you *buy* predictable lead flow once the messaging works.
2. **Copy & creative are the real targeting** post-iOS14 — so the **stages-of-awareness** model and the **four frameworks** aren't just for ads; they upgrade every message in the whole knowledge base (content hooks, DMs, cold emails, VSLs, pages).
3. **Account structure** (Persona/ABO → Angle/CBO) and the **Ad Grid** turn messaging into a systematic, testable production line.
4. **Email + automation** convert the opt-ins paid traffic produces, using the same frameworks and the funnel plumbing from File 2.

**The single biggest idea:** since the platforms can no longer target for you, **the message is the targeting.** Win by (a) knowing your prospect's stage of awareness, (b) writing to it with a proven framework, (c) systematising that into an ad grid and a persona/angle account structure, and (d) letting email + automation compound each opt-in into a client. Spend scales results only as far as your copy and offer can carry colder traffic — so messaging, not budget, is the real lever.
