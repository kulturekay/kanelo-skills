# Niche & Target Market

> **Section 2 of the "How to Build a Service-Based Business" knowledge base.**
> Section 1 (*Service and Niche*) answered *what* to build and the broad *who*. This section gets specific: it defines your **IACP — your Ideal Active Client Profile** (exactly who you target and why), and then the **messaging** you use to convert that person from a stranger into a paying client.

The core idea: **it's not the service that sells — it's the message.** You can have the best offer in the world, but if it isn't communicated in a way that resonates with a clearly-defined ideal client, it won't convert. Most founders pick a niche, slap on a generic strapline, fire off outreach, and then stall at ~$10k/month. The fix is two-fold — *know exactly who you're talking to* (Part A), then *say the right thing to them* (Part B).

---

# Part A — Defining Your IACP (Ideal Active Client Profile)

The clearer you are about your ideal client, the easier they are to reach, connect with, and convert. The IACP is a worksheet you fill in for each candidate client *archetype* so your strategy speaks directly to their needs and goals.

## The IACP worksheet — fields to define

For every ideal-client archetype, answer:

1. **Client name / example** — a real company or creator that fits the profile.
2. **What industry are they in?**
3. **Location** — where they are / your ideal target locations.
4. **Size of business:**
   - **Small** — 1–20 people (startup)
   - **Medium** — 20–100 people (SMB)
   - **Large** — 100+ people (corporate)
5. **What products or services do they sell?**
6. **Key relevant problems** they're likely having *that your service solves* — this is the heart of the profile.
7. **Approx budget** you think they have (per month).
8. **Who are the founders?**
9. **Who do they follow on socials?** — reveals their influences, where they pay attention, and how to reach/credentialise to them.
10. **Long-term business potential** — low / medium / high opportunity for an ongoing relationship.
11. **Personality fit** — would *you* actually work well with them? (Yes / No)

> Tip from Section 1: identify **3 IACPs** to confirm there are enough real clients you can credibly service before committing to the niche.

## How the IACP scales by client size

The worksheet is filled across a ladder of client sizes, the same ladder as the messaging segments in Part B. As you move up the ladder, budgets rise — **but so does the risk of being replaced by an in-house hire or a big-name agency.** A recurring pattern in the examples: above a certain budget threshold a client *"would just hire in-house"* or *"go to the Neil Patels / Sharma Brands of the world who charge $30k/m minimum."* That defines a **sweet-spot budget band** for a startup agency — big enough to pay well, small enough that the client won't replace you with a team or a market leader.

## What the IACP exercise teaches

- **Profile by problem, not by demographic.** The value of each profile is the *specific problem your service solves* for that client. That problem becomes the hook of your messaging.
- **Budget bands reveal the sweet spot.** Read the "they'd just hire in-house / go to a big agency above $X" note for each — that's the ceiling, and just under it is where a startup agency wins.
- **Influences are a roadmap.** "Who they follow" tells you whose names to drop, where to show up, and what content style they already trust.
- **Fit is a filter, not an afterthought.** "Good personality fit?" matters: a client you click with becomes a long-term, referenceable relationship; one you don't becomes churn.
- **Long-term potential ranks the list.** Prioritise high-potential, good-fit clients in the sweet-spot budget band.

# Part B — Master Messaging to Sign Your Dream Clients

Once you know *who* (the IACP), you craft *what you say*. The materials use one running example throughout:

> **ShortFormPro** — a content-repurposing agency for podcasts. **Service:** turn podcast episodes into viral short-form videos. **Core offer:** 10 high-quality edited short-form videos per episode, distributed across 5+ channels weekly. **Target clients:** podcast creators, media brands, DTC founders with podcasts.

## The messaging formula: CLAIM → APPROACH → PROOF → MARKET

A 4-part framework for a clear, credible, compelling message that hits the right nerve.

### 1. CLAIM — the result you promise
Focus on the **outcome**, not the service. Short, specific, problem-solving. Ask: *"What's the #1 result my ideal client desperately wants?"*

- ❌ "We make videos for your podcast."
- ✅ "We help podcasts like yours grow across TikTok, IG Reels and YouTube Shorts by a minimum 15% in the first month using our viral b-roll collection."

**Fill-in prompt (write 3):** *"We help [target audience] like you [accomplish X] [as measured by Y] [by doing Z]."*

### 2. APPROACH — how you deliver that result
Be specific about your method. No jargon, no buzzword-hiding. Describe the process step by step. Ask: *"How exactly do I deliver this outcome?"*

- ❌ "We create engaging short-form content."
- ✅ "We take your full episodes, pick the most viral clips, edit them with motion graphics, and post daily across 5 platforms — without you lifting a finger."

**Build it in 3 parts:** (1) What you do when you get a client → (2) How you execute the service → (3) How you ensure results / improvement over time.

### 3. PROOF — why they should trust you
Use real metrics, case studies, screenshots, logos, testimonials. Ask: *"What have I done that proves I can do this?"*

- ❌ "We're really good at this."
- ✅ "We helped The Growth Pod hit 1M views across TikTok and IG in 60 days — 70% more than their previous month."

**If you're new:** use mock projects, your own personal-brand growth, or **"permissionless proof"** — make a free sample and say *"I created this clip for you to show what's possible."*

**Gather:** 2 real outcomes/numbers, 1 client quote, 1 platform where you've personally succeeded.

### 4. MARKET — why this matters *now*
Tie the service to current trends/urgency, show what peers are doing (peer pressure), hint at the cost of missing out. Ask: *"What's happening in the market that makes this urgent?"*

- ❌ "This is a great service."
- ✅ "Podcasts have 40% more listeners this year, yet only 12% of creators distribute across Shorts, IG and TikTok — cross-platform growth is easier now than it was 6 months ago."

**Turn it into a sentence:** *"Because [market shift], [your solution] is the smart move for [target audience]."*

## Adapting the message by client type

The same offer is framed differently per segment (these map directly to Section 1's business-size segments and to the IACP ladder):

| Segment | Claim | Approach | Proof | Market |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Freelancer** (solo podcaster) | Land 10k+ new views/week | 10 viral clips/episode across 5 platforms | One creator gained 8k followers in 30 days | More podcasters going viral than ever — non-repurposers fall behind |
| **Startup** (SaaS founder w/ podcast) | Turn your podcast into an email list | Each episode → 10+ clips + lead magnets | Added 1,500 subs/mo at 5% conversion for a SaaS | Working for [competitor] growing 18% MoM |
| **SMB** (media co / agency) | 3× your video output without hiring in-house | Integrate with your team, weekly batch across brands | Scaled an agency to 60+ videos/mo across 4 clients, no added cost | Freelancers are a pain — our setup feels in-house |
| **Corporate** (large podcast network) | Scale brand awareness via enterprise-grade repurposing | Platform-native edits via structured workflows + team integration | 200+ videos/mo for a 12-show network → 10M Shorts views, +30% MoM | TikTok is now the #1 podcast-discovery channel for Gen Z |

> Notice the escalation: freelancers buy on **emotion and speed**, startups on **scale and growth**, SMBs on **efficiency and proof**, corporates on **enterprise scale and market positioning** — exactly mirroring the segment behaviours from Section 1.

## Adapting the message by funnel stage

| Funnel stage | Message intent | ShortFormPro example |
|---|---|---|
| **Top (Awareness)** | Name the problem, promise relief | "Struggling to grow your podcast? Let your content work for you." |
| **Middle (Consideration)** | Show the mechanism + scale of result | "Here's how we turn your podcast into 50+ videos/month and grow reach 10×." |
| **Bottom (Conversion)** | Concrete proof + "same for you" | "Client X got 2.4M views in 6 weeks. We'll build the same system for you." |

## The messaging worksheet (one consolidated message)

| Section | Example |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | "We help podcasters grow 5× faster by turning episodes into short-form videos." |
| **Approach** | "We clip, edit, and post 10 videos from every episode across TikTok, IG, YouTube Shorts and Twitter." |
| **Proof** | "Grew @GrowthPod from 1.2k to 30k followers in 8 weeks." |
| **Market** | "Short-form is the #1 discovery channel for podcasts — you're missing reach without it." |

## Execution checklist — before you hit send

- Does this message solve a **specific problem**?
- Is the **approach** clearly explained?
- Have I included **proof** (numbers, stories, screenshots)?
- Is there **urgency / market context**?
- **Would I reply** if I were the client?

## Applying it to outreach

Every message — cold email, DM, or pitch deck — should follow the same structure:
**Claim** (what you offer) → **Approach** (how you deliver) → **Proof** (why believe you) → **Market** (why now).

Most outreach fails because it (1) talks about the agency instead of the client, (2) makes generic promises with no credibility, or (3) skips the *how* — which is the part that actually builds interest. The framework above fixes all three.

## Synthesis — how this section connects

The decisions stack on top of Section 1:

1. **You chose a service path and agency type** (Section 1).
2. **Define your IACP** (Part A): profile real client archetypes by industry, size, products, *problems your service solves*, budget, influences, long-term potential, and personality fit. Identify your **3 IACPs** in the **sweet-spot budget band** — below the "they'd hire in-house / use a big agency" ceiling.
3. **Craft your message** (Part B): for each IACP, build a **Claim → Approach → Proof → Market** message, then **adapt it by segment** (freelancer/startup/SMB/corporate) and by **funnel stage** (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU).
4. **Run the checklist**, then deploy it through outreach.

**The single biggest idea of this section:** specificity converts. A profile that names the exact client, their exact problem, their exact budget ceiling, and their exact influences — paired with a message that promises a specific result, explains the specific method, proves it with specific numbers, and grounds it in a specific market shift — makes your offer feel *obvious* to the right person. Generic targeting + generic messaging is why most agencies stall; precise targeting + precise messaging is how you sign dream clients.
