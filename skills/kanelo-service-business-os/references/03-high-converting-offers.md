# High-Converting Offers

> **Section 3 of the "How to Build a Service-Based Business" knowledge base.**
> Section 1 (*Service and Niche*) chose *what* to build; Section 2 (*Niche & Target Market*) defined *who* you serve and *what you say* to them. This section is the final piece of the foundation: **how you structure and price the offer** so it's profitable, competitive, and built around the specific client in front of you.

The core idea: a high-converting offer is **custom, profitable, and tied to outcomes.** You build it one of two ways — from your costs up (*bottom-up*) or from the market down (*top-down*) — and you can add a performance layer that aligns your earnings with the client's success.

---

## The two levers of every offer

Every offer is built from two components. You can use one or both.

1. **Base Cost** — covers your service costs plus a profit margin. Forms:
   - *One-off projects* — e.g. $10k for a website build.
   - *Hourly / flexible services* — e.g. $10/hour for ongoing customer service.
   - *Contracted retainers* — e.g. $1.5k/month for 2 YouTube videos.
2. **Performance-Based Compensation** — a results incentive on top of the base. Forms:
   - *Revenue percentage* — e.g. 20% of AdSense revenue from YouTube videos.
   - *Deals closed* — e.g. 10% commission on new business closed.
   - *Profit share* — e.g. 20% of profit from Meta Ads.

### The two offer structures

| Structure | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Base only** | **Simple Offer** | A set fee with clear deliverables. |
| **Base + Performance** | **Partner / Premium Offer** | A set fee combined with a results-based incentive. |

The framework is designed to let you **start simple and scale**: begin with a straightforward Base offer, then graduate clients to a Base + Performance model for greater earning potential and deeper client alignment.

---

## Approach 1 — Bottom-Up (cost → price)

Start from what it costs *you* to deliver, then add margin. This guarantees every offer is profitable.

### Steps to calculate your base offer

1. **Estimate your base price.** Total cost of delivering the service + a profit margin. *The written guide recommends aiming for ~50% margin to ensure profitability.* (Note: the offer calculator spreadsheet uses 30% — pick a margin and apply it consistently; see the calculator section below.)
2. **Break down costs.** Include the real cost per hour of your (or your team's) time on client work, meetings, and deliverables, plus software, office, and admin costs.
3. **Apply the formula:**
   > **Hourly Rate × Hours for Project + Additional Costs = Total Cost**
4. **Add your margin** to get the price.

**Worked example (from the guide):**
- $50/hour, project takes 40 hours, $500 in software costs.
- Cost = ($50 × 40) + $500 = **$2,500**
- With 50% margin = $2,500 + $1,250 = **$3,750 base price**

### Adjust for your target market
- **Smaller / B2C markets** → lower base, less complexity (e.g. $3k for email marketing for a new clothing brand).
- **Larger / B2B markets** → higher base, more complexity (e.g. $10k for email marketing for a brand like Nike).

## Approach 2 — Top-Down (market → price)

Start from the market and competitors, then position your price where it captures the most value.

1. **Look at the market**
   - *Spot trends* — what's in high demand, where new needs are emerging.
   - *Check competitor pricing* — what similar services cost (top competitors *and* the average).
   - *Find service gaps* — high demand + few providers = where you stand out.
2. **Set profit goals**
   - *Calculate margins* from your costs and competitor pricing.
   - *Compare benchmarks* — set a profit goal that meets or beats industry standards after delivery, marketing, and other costs.
3. **Consider upsell opportunities**
   - *Premium pricing* — charge more where demand is high and your service is unique/high-quality.
   - *Add extra services* — faster delivery, ongoing support, or extra features justify a higher price.
4. **Find your "grey area"**
   - *Locate the middle ground* — the range between low-budget and premium, where customers see the best value.
   - *Highlight your USPs* — why pick you over cheaper or pricier options (faster results, measurable outcomes, better quality).

> **Bottom-up sets your floor; top-down sets your ceiling.** Use both: bottom-up tells you the lowest profitable price, top-down tells you what the market will bear. Your offer lives in the overlap — the "grey area."

## Adding the Performance layer

Performance compensation is what turns a Simple Offer into a Premium/Partner Offer and differentiates you from competitors.

### Define the right metrics
- Tie incentives to **outcomes, not leads.** Use conversion metrics (closed sales, ROI from ad spend) — **not** lead volume, which creates misalignment over lead quality.
- Anchor the incentive on **your unique skill or expertise** that directly drives results, rather than promising a fixed number of leads.

### Worked example — ads client
- Client spends **$20k/month** on Meta + Google ads and wants **2× ROI**.
- *Standard offer:* $5k/month retainer.
- *Performance offer instead:* **$3k/month base + 20% of ad profit.**
- **Month 1:** $20k profit on their ads → agency earns $3k + $4k (20% of $20k) = **$7k total** (vs. $5k flat).
- **By month 3:** scaled their account to $40k spend, earning the agency **~$20k+/month.**

### Why Base + Performance wins
- **Incentivises quality** — your earnings rise with the client's success, so you stay motivated.
- **Differentiates you** — it signals confidence and a commitment to measurable outcomes.
- **Scales with the client** — as you grow their results, you grow your own revenue without renegotiating a flat fee.

## The Offer Calculator

A simple model that turns your income target into a per-client price. Logic, using the worked example (weekly YouTube content for a new client):

| Input | Value | How it's derived |
|---|---|---|
| Target income this year (cost of the main person doing the service) | **$100,000** | Your goal |
| Your hourly rate | **$48.08** | $100,000 ÷ 2,080 avg working hours/year |
| Time delivering this service | 1 day/week = 8 hrs | → $384.62/week → **$1,538.46/month** (8 × rate × 4) |
| Freelancers on this client | Thumbnail designer $500 + Editor $500 | **$1,000/month** |
| Agency overhead (Dropbox, Slack, Webflow, Calendly @ $20 each) | $80 total | Split across 3 clients → **$26.67/client** |
| **Total cost to service this client** | **$2,565.13** | labour + freelancers + overhead share |
| Margin (30% in the calculator) | **$769.54** | $2,565.13 × 30% |
| **Price to charge** | **$3,334.67** | cost + margin |
| **Marketing-friendly number** | **$3,500** | rounded to a clean figure |

**The calculator's logic in words:** *(your monthly labour at your hourly rate) + (freelancer costs for this client) + (this client's share of fixed overhead) = total cost. Multiply by your margin, then round up to a clean number you'll quote.* Note the margin discrepancy between the written guide (~50%) and the spreadsheet (30%) — decide your standard and apply it everywhere.

## Key Principles

- **No money-back guarantee.** Guarantees don't suit the agency model. Demonstrate value and deliver strong, results-oriented service instead.
- **Custom offers only — no fixed packages.** Build each offer around the exact deliverables the client requests.
  - *Example:* if a client wants **4 YouTube videos/month**, don't present 3/5/10-video tiers — give an exact quote for **4 videos** using your pricing formula (with your margin baked in).
- **Pricing flexibility.** Because every offer is custom, you can adjust on deliverables and negotiate where needed while protecting profitability.
- **Keep every option profitable** — including downsells and upsells.
  - *Downsell:* a smaller package (fewer deliverables) for a limited budget.
  - *Upsell:* extra or premium services when the client wants to expand scope.

## Synthesis — how this section connects

The offer is the last item on Section 1's launch checklist, and it's built directly on Sections 1 and 2:

1. **From Section 2's IACP** you know the client's **size, budget band, and the specific problem** your service solves — those set whether you're pricing B2C-simple or B2B-complex, and where the "they'd hire in-house" ceiling sits.
2. **Bottom-up** gives your profitable floor (cost + margin); **top-down** gives the market ceiling (trends, competitor pricing, gaps). Price in the **grey area** between them.
3. **Add a performance layer** tied to real outcomes (ROI, profit, closed sales — never lead volume) to turn a Simple Offer into a Premium/Partner Offer that scales your revenue with the client's results.
4. **Quote it custom** — exact deliverables, your margin baked in, no fixed packages, no money-back guarantee, downsell/upsell paths that all stay profitable.

**The single biggest idea:** structure the offer so you win when the client wins. A custom base covers your costs and margin with certainty; a performance share aligns you with the client's success and uncaps your upside. That combination converts better than a flat retainer *and* earns more over time.
