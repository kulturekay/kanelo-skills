# Sales Leads and Pipeline Management

> **Client Acquisition · File 6 (the capstone).**
> Files 1–5 each cover a way to *generate* leads — content, funnels, hot outreach, cold outreach, paid ads. This file is where they all converge: the **single sales funnel and pipeline** that takes every lead, regardless of source, through one repeatable process from first touch to signed client to handover. It also covers the **CRM tracker**, the **team roles** at each stage, the **qualification tiers**, and the **service agreement** that formally closes a deal.
>
> **Automation note (carried through):** every operational step here — sourcing, qualifying, CRM updates, follow-up scheduling, stage movement, reporting — is executable by a **VA or an AI agent.** Read "VA" as "VA or AI agent."

The core idea: **lead generation is plural, but the pipeline is singular.** You can pour leads in from eight different channels, but they all flow into *one* funnel with *one* set of stages — and managing that pipeline (not just filling it) is what turns leads into revenue.

## 1. The Agency Sales Funnel — where every channel converges

The funnel has three vertical layers:

### Layer 1 — Lead Gen (top, widest)
**All eight lead sources feed in here** — this is the entire Client Acquisition section in one row:
- DMs, lead magnets & tactics *(File 1)*
- Personal brand *(File 1)*
- Paid ads *(File 5)*

- Referrals *(new — see §6)*
- Funnels, landing pages, VSLs *(File 2)*
- Automated cold outreach *(File 4)*

### Layer 2 — Sales (middle, narrowing)
Once a lead is generated, it moves through the sales motion:
**Outreach → Sales calls → Relationship → Pitch → Negotiate.**
This whole layer is governed by **Pipeline Management** (the discipline of §2–3) and is where the *Sign Clients Like Never Before* deck + two-call framework operates.

### Layer 3 — Signed → Service Delivery (bottom, narrowest)
The lead becomes **Signed**, then exits the acquisition funnel into **Service Delivery** (the next knowledge section — fulfilment).

## 2. The Sales System — the pipeline flow

The operational pipeline a single lead travels through:

```
Source → Outreach → Response → Pitch → Post-pitch follow-up
       → Proposal → Agreement → Signed
```

With branches handling reality:

- **Follow-up loop:** after Outreach, if no response → **Day 2 → Day 5 → Day 10 follow-ups** (Day 2 = continue angle, Day 5 = new angle, Day 10 = continue Day-5). Each follow-up either continues the angle or tries a different one.
- **Dead → reactivation:** if all follow-ups fail, the lead goes **Dead** — but it's **not gone forever**. After **3 months it reappears** and re-enters Outreach. Nothing is wasted; cold leads are recycled on a timer.
- **Proposal sub-states:** **Proposal Needed → Proposal Sent.**
- **Infrastructure:** use your **own inbox**; you **can do links** (tracked booking/asset links).

> The two design features that matter most: **(1) disciplined timed follow-ups** (Day 2/5/10) so leads aren't dropped, and **(2) the Dead→3-month→reappear loop** so a "no" today becomes a fresh opportunity next quarter. A lead is never truly lost — only paused.

## 3. Pipeline Management in the CRM (Pipedrive)

The pipeline is run as a **Pipedrive board**, one column per stage, every lead a card showing name, handle, owner, and deal value. The observed stage columns (a real agency pipeline):

**Sourced → Outreached → Day 2 → Day 5 → Day 10 → Responded → Meeting Requested → Meeting Confirmed → Intro Meeting → Post Meeting → Pitch Needed → Pitch Ready → Business Plan/Pitch → Pitch Sent → Negotiations → Agreement → (Signed).**

Why this matters operationally:
- **Every lead has a stage and a value** — so you can see total pipeline value and weighted forecast at a glance (the example board showed **451 deals / ~£30M pipeline / ~£7.3M weighted**).
- **Stages = the follow-up system encoded** — Day 2/5/10 are literal columns, so a VA/agent just works the columns left-to-right each day.
- **Cards carry the owner** — accountability is visible (ties to the EOD report in *Outbound — Hot Outreach*).
- **Daily update is mandatory** — the CRM is the single source of truth for the whole acquisition team.

> **AI-agent translation:** an agent maintains the board — creating cards on Source, advancing them through Outreach/Day 2/5/10 on schedule, moving Responded deals to the pitch team, flagging "Pitch Needed," logging proposal/agreement states, and recycling Dead cards after 3 months — emitting the EOD metrics straight from the board.

## 4. The Acquisition Process — five phases and who owns each

End-to-end, the acquisition process is **Find → Qualify → Pitch → Deal → Onboard**, each with a clear owner so a lead is never "between" people.

| Phase | What happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Find (Sourcing)** | Inbound leads (web/social), outreach, referrals (team), referrals (clients & 3rd-party partners) | **Outreach team** (Mgr + Execs) |
| **Qualify (Lead Qualification)** | Score the lead against criteria (good vs bad — see §5) | **Outreach team** (Mgr + Execs) |
| **Pitch (Pitch Phase)** | Plan what you can do for the lead · decide who from the team adds most value · build a **bespoke pitch** for the initial call · practice and plan exactly how to run it · pitch on the call · send a **personal follow-up** · **make the next step clear** | **Pitch team** (CEO + Value + Ops) |
| **Deal (Deal Phase)** | **Ownership transfers to whoever led the pitch** — they stay on top of it · keep creating info/decks to get the client over the line · set up a meeting to discuss deal details, answer questions, gather any further info · carry out all actions to reach agreement and get the client to sign · *critical: the outreach executive stays on top of the deal* | **Pitch team** (CEO + Value + Ops) |
| **Onboard (Account Management)** | Once a newly signed client is handed to the brand/service team they're assigned an **AM** · the new client + plans are announced internally · **book the onboarding call** | **Services team** (Account Manager) |

> **The handoff logic:** the *outreach team* finds and qualifies; the *pitch team* (led by a senior closer) pitches and closes; the *services team* onboards and delivers. Ownership is explicit at each boundary — and during the Deal phase, the person who *led the pitch* owns it through to signature, so nothing falls between pitching and closing.

## 5. Client Qualification — the tiers, problems, and fees

Before a lead is worth pitching, it's qualified on quality *and* sized for fee potential. Two screens:

### Universal qualifying criteria (every lead, regardless of tier)
- **Activity & visibility:** story up? posting consistently? best times? talks in stories/highlights? markets themselves well? uses other platforms?
- **Niche & authority:** has a niche? can you tell what it is quickly? actively promotes it? comes off knowledgeable? commands attention? owns their page with confidence?
- **Likeability & engagement:** authentic? easy to like/relate to? real person? audience invested? good caption/Q&A/reply use?
- **Passion & willingness to invest:** effort into their page/content? do they have the capital?
- **Hard analytics gates:** **≥50% same-gender audience · ≥3% engagement rate · ≥5% avg monthly growth.** *(Expert tip: discount for a high % of fake followers when estimating real reach.)*

### The three tiers (who they are → problems → fee potential)

| Tier | Who they are | Problems they face | Fee potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mega** | Large creator, millions of followers; already makes money; lifestyle/fashion/education; wants to build something of their own or scale | "Not sure what business to start"; "worried about selling out to their audience" | **$500k+/year** |
| **Macro** | 250k–700k IG/TT or 150k–500k YT; ~£100k–£800k revenue; passionate, niche, loyal following, proven ability to sell; some capital + willingness to invest | "Need cashflow to chase their dreams"; "need to learn skills to improve" | **$250k+/year** |
| **Micro** | <250k IG/TT or <150k YT; revenue <£100k; serious potential but up-and-coming; likely little capital; could generate revenue if self-serve or managed | "Need to grow their audience"; "need to quit their job/school" | **$100k+/year** |

> This tiering connects directly to the **IACP** (*Niche & Target Market*) and **Lead Analysis buckets** (*Sign Clients* / *Outbound — Hot Outreach*) — it adds the **fee band** so you can forecast deal value the moment a lead is qualified, which is exactly what populates the Pipedrive card value in §3.

## 6. Two lead sources that live only in the pipeline view

The funnel (§1) names two sources not detailed in Files 1–5:

- **Referrals** — from the team, from existing clients, and from 3rd-party partners. Named explicitly in the *Find* phase (§4). Typically the highest-trust, fastest-closing leads.
- **Partner agencies / middle-men** — other agencies or intermediaries who pass deals (e.g. work they can't service, or in exchange for a cut). A B2B lead channel that complements direct outreach.

> Both are low-volume but high-quality. They enter the *same* pipeline at the *same* stages — proving the "many sources, one funnel" principle.

## 7. Closing the deal — the Service Agreement

"Signed" becomes real when a **service agreement** is executed. This is the document that converts a won deal into a live client and protects both sides. The template's key clauses:

- **Parties** — full legal entities of agency and client (company numbers, registered addresses).
- **Background & The Service** — who you are, what the partnership is, and a detailed definition of what's being provided.
- **Client website/social link** — to unambiguously identify the client.
- **Effective date** — date of client signature.
- **Agency services** — exactly what you'll deliver.
- **Client obligations** — what the client must provide (e.g. direction/feedback within X business days; content needed to run ads).
- **Fee, profit & accounting** — the fee, how often and how you're paid (deduct from profit / invoice net-14 / etc.), who covers other costs; all sums become **immediately due on termination**.
- **Limitation of liability** — caps total liability (except death/injury by negligence, fraud, the indemnity); excludes indirect/consequential loss, loss of goodwill/business/data.
- **Exclusivity** — client won't engage another provider for the same/similar service without written agreement.
- **Non-disparagement** — neither party (nor their staff) disparages the other.
- **IP rights** — define Client IP vs Agency IP separately.
- **Term** — initial term + auto-renewal, with X days' written notice to stop.
- **Termination** — immediate for non-payment (14-day cure), material/irremediable breach (30-day cure), repeated breach, or insolvency.
- **Reputation & conduct** — agency may terminate immediately if the client's public conduct damages the agency's reputation.
- **Obligations on termination** — return property/materials, erase confidential info, certify compliance, hand over Client IP.
- **Notices** — by email, deemed received at transmission (or 9am next business day if out of hours).
- **Confidential information & confidentiality** — define it; survives **2 years** post-termination.
- **Data protection / customer data** — client is **data controller**, agency is **data processor**.
- **Governing law & jurisdiction** — set the country/courts (template defaults to UK).
- **Signatures** — both parties; agreement effective on the last signature.

> **Why it belongs in pipeline management:** the agreement is the final pipeline stage made legal. It's what "Agreement → Signed" actually *is*. Standardising it means every closed deal is consistent, protected, and fast to execute — no bespoke legal drafting per client. *(Not legal advice — have a qualified lawyer review and adapt the template for your jurisdiction before use.)*

## Synthesis — how this ties the whole section together

This file is the **spine** of Client Acquisition:

1. **All five lead-gen engines** (Files 1–5) plus **referrals and partner agencies** feed **one funnel** (§1).
2. That funnel runs as a **managed pipeline** — the Sales System flow (§2) encoded as **Pipedrive stages** (§3), worked by a VA/AI agent left-to-right daily, with **Day 2/5/10 follow-ups** and a **Dead→3-month→reappear** recycle so no lead is lost.
3. The pipeline is executed by **three teams across five phases** — Outreach (Find/Qualify) → Pitch (Pitch/Deal) → Services (Onboard) — with **explicit ownership** at every handoff (§4).
4. **Qualification tiers** (Mega/Macro/Micro) attach a **fee band** to each lead so the pipeline forecasts revenue, not just volume (§5).
5. A standardised **service agreement** converts the final stage into a signed, protected client (§7), who then exits into **Service Delivery** (the next section).

**The single biggest idea:** generating leads is necessary but not sufficient — **the money is made in *managing* the pipeline.** Many channels, one funnel; every lead has a stage, an owner, and a value; follow-ups are systematic and dead leads are recycled; ownership transfers cleanly from finder to closer to account manager; and a standard agreement makes "Signed" repeatable. A business that manages its pipeline this tightly can scale lead-gen on any channel and trust the same machine to convert it.
