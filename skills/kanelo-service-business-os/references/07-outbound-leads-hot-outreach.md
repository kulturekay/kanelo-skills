# Outbound Leads — Hot Outreach

> **Client Acquisition · File 3.**
> *Inbound Leads* (File 1) pulls clients toward you with content; *Landing Pages, Funnels & VSLs* (File 2) catches and converts that demand. This file is the **outbound engine** — proactively sourcing, qualifying, and personally reaching out to the exact clients you want, at volume but never at the expense of quality.
>
> **Automation note:** this system was originally designed to run on **VAs (virtual assistants)**. Everywhere a VA performs a step below, **an AI agent should be able to perform the same step** — sourcing, qualifying, enriching, drafting personalised outreach, logging to the CRM, scheduling follow-ups, and reporting. Read every "VA" in this document as "**VA or AI agent**." The workflow, criteria, and templates are written to be executable by either.

The core idea: **hot outreach is really custom, 100% manual-quality, value-first outreach at scale.** Quality over quantity — but with enough system, tooling, and people/agents behind it that quality *becomes* quantity.

---

## 1. What hot outreach is (the philosophy)

Hot outreach sits at the top of the outbound system. Its defining traits:

- **Really custom outreach** — every message is specific to the recipient.
- **Quality over quantity** — a few great messages beat a blast of generic ones.
- **It's 100% manual** (in quality) — even when scaled with VAs/agents, each message reads as if hand-written by a person who did their research.
- **Use VAs/AI agents** to scale the manual work — find leads, write from script templates, send value-based personal emails.
- **Built on a decision tree** — qualification routes each lead to the right angle/template.
- **Send the perfect thing to solve their problem** — and **manage each response yourself** (or via a trained agent following the decision tree).
- **Constant training + constant optimisation** — the system improves continuously.

### The hot-outreach flow

```
   Know someone ┐
 Downloaded list ├──→  QUALIFY  ──→  ANGLE  ──→  HOT OUTREACH
 Found manually  ┘                  (Problem /
                                     Service)
```

A lead enters from one of three sources, gets **qualified**, is matched to an **angle** (the problem you'll lead with + the service you'll offer), and only then receives the hot-outreach message.

## 2. Sourcing leads

Three ways to find leads for hot outreach:

1. **Manually** — hand-picked while browsing social platforms / the web.
2. **Scrape** — Email + LinkedIn scraping.
3. **Get leads from platforms** — pull from lead databases/platforms.

**Volume targets (per VA/agent):**
- **Source 30–50 leads/day** (1 VA/agent should be finding this many).
- **Send a minimum of 10–20 emails/day.**

> The number is meaningful only because the qualification step (next) filters hard. Sourcing volume × strict qualification = a clean pipeline of *worth-it* leads.

## 3. Qualifying leads

Qualification has two layers: the **quick decision-tree qualification** (good/bad lead at a glance) and a **deep, criteria-based qualification** for high-value targets.

### Layer 1 — Good vs Bad (the Lead Analysis buckets)

Every lead is sorted into **Fake / Good / Bad / High Potential** using the Lead Analysis worksheet (detailed in *Sign Clients Like Never Before* and *Niche & Target Market*). For each lead, a VA/agent fills:
1. Client name
2. IACP? (do they fit topline?)
3. How legit are they? (web, founder, socials, founder socials, LinkedIn, Google)
4. Size of business (web traffic, employees, industry tools, signs of success)
5. Who is the lead person? (their role)
6. What problem do they want solved? (reuse IACP work)
7. What service do they want?
8. What offer would work? (rough $/month)

Prioritise **High Potential**, then **Good**; drop **Fake** and **Bad**.

### Layer 2 — Deep qualification criteria (worked example: a fitness-app creator)

For high-value verticals, run a structured checklist before outreach. This is the exact model used for qualifying fitness creators — adapt the specifics to your niche, keep the structure:

**Pre-checks / housekeeping**
- Are they a current/previous client? Are they on the blacklist? (check all relevant client lists)
- Basic Google search — how are they famous? Any recent news?
- Are they a true creator (famous via IG/TikTok/YouTube)?
- Where are they based? Can you work with them given team logistics?
- Have you outreached them before in the CRM (Pipedrive)? What's the deal history — can you re-approach?

**Growth (via analytics tools, e.g. "Genlytics")**
- Followers increasing YoY / MoM across platforms?
- Followers increase when they post more content?
- **Audience growth rate >20%**, and **~70% of months show positive growth.**

**Audience-to-market fit**
- Gender breakdown: relevant audience is **same sex as the creator, +60%.**
- Age breakdown: audience age matches the creator, no odd outliers.
- Country breakdown: highest or second-highest is a relevant (workable) country.

**Content**
- **≥2% engagement rate.**
- Comments are related to their niche content.
- Posts across IG, YouTube *and* TikTok, and posts regularly.
- Posts add genuine value.

**"About the creator" depth checks** (niche-specific — fitness example)
- Face-to-camera content? Workout routines? Pre-workout? Meal prep? Other niche topics? Before/afters (if selling programs)? Non-niche posts — do they still engage well?

**Partnerships**
- Which brands have they partnered with — relevant to the niche?
- Do they regularly post partnerships/products relevant to their audience (e.g. supplements, activewear)?

**The fast qualification gate (must-pass screen):**
- It's a **real person/creator** — not a meme/review/gossip/sports account.
- They make content for the **same sex as them** (looking for e.g. female creators with a larger male following, and vice versa).
- They **speak on camera** on some platform → must be **YES.**
- They live in an **English-speaking country.**
- **Minimum 250k followers.**
- **At least 1% engagement.**
- *Then* search on the analytics tool; if they're a strong ("MEGA") fit, pick the best monetisation method, check screening questions, and select the template.

> **Why two layers:** Layer 1 stops you wasting time on obviously bad leads in seconds. Layer 2 makes sure that before you spend real effort personalising outreach to a high-value target, they genuinely fit — on growth, audience, content, and commercial potential.

## 4. The outreach itself

### Hot outreach template (3 sections)

**Section 1 — Peak Interest (the problem you found)**
1. State the problem — *"your website is slow," "your podcast clips are edited poorly," "your funnel is flawed."*
2. Mention something non-obvious about them — *"I listened to what you said in X," "you have a TikTok account but no TikTok Shop," "you're not using email automations."*
3. Add a genuine connection — *"I also love X," "that UFC fight yesterday was great."*

**Section 2 — The Value (what makes you the expert)**
Give them the answer *now*. Whatever you'd want them to pay for, hand over a piece of it free:
- *"Switch your email to long-form and you'll easily see a 20% open-rate difference."*
- *"I re-edited one of your videos — here it is, and why I did XYZ."*
- *"Based on your engagement and traffic, a TikTok Shop would likely sell ~2k orders/month."*

**Section 3 — About you + CTA**
- *"I'm X from Y. I've done Z. I think I can help here."*
- **Always end with a question** — never *"let me know"* or *"let's get a call."* → *"What do you think of your short-form content?"* / *"Have you bought from TikTok Shop?"*

**Worked example — paid-ads agency → a brand not running ads:**
> *"Hi X, I bought your T-shirt and love the material — been wearing it constantly. I was browsing the site and noticed you don't run any ads. I think you could kill it with UGC showing the product being worn, as it sells itself. I know because I run an agency that does this for brands — done it for X, Y, Z. On average we see cost-per-acquisition around $7, which would help you scale. Have you run ads, or is there a reason you haven't started?"*

### Hot follow-ups
Same structure, **rotate the angle:** Problem → Solution · Opportunity → Money · Personal connection → Trust · Highlight competitors → "you can do this too."

### Cold outreach (automated to a pulled list)
Tighter targeting, merge-field personalisation. Example criteria for a paid-ads play: *D2C brand · >10k visits/month · >10 employees on LinkedIn · problem: not running ads (or doing them poorly).*

> *"Hi {{firstName}}, came across {{companyName}} last night while shopping. Love the messaging. I noticed you don't use many paid ads. I run agency X — organic-style try-on videos, unboxings and product reviews would do really well for you. Would love to help you scale. What's the current strategy?"*

Two more worked angles in the materials: a **CRO-agency founder** ("you used to post but went inactive — massive missed opportunity… here's an example… ~2 hrs/week for 7 posts") and a **D2C clothing brand** ("got hit with your ad… I'd switch to an agreeable-statement hook… what's your paid-media plan for Q4?"). Both end on a question; the personal-brand one adds a genuine **PS** (e.g. "who do you think wins at UFC XX?").

### Cold follow-up cadence
- **Day 2** — continue the same angle.
- **Day 5** — a *new* angle / fresh email.
- **Day 10** — continue the Day-5 angle.

**Never say:** *"just following up," "did you get a chance to check this," "just wanted to know if you're interested."*

## 5. Stop / Start / Continue — the rules of good outreach

A retro-style summary of what separates outreach that works from outreach that gets ignored.

### 🔴 STOP — what people do wrong
- Never use gimmicks; no fake things; no fake scarcity.
- Didn't do any research at all.
- Don't name-drop random people unless they're truly tier-1.
- Never send portfolios (unprompted).
- Misleading subject lines; "bullshit" metrics.
- Different-industry examples are weak — use relevant ones.
- Don't push for a negative response ("opt-out video," etc.).
- Don't be selling something you don't actually do.
- **Never scrape and email more than one person per company in a single email.**

### 🔵 START — what to do instead
- Emails, comments, DMs (show up across channels).
- Show off your skills *in the template itself.*
- Go for "home-run" clients.
- Use **only real numbers that mean something.**
- Put **$ values wherever you can.**
- Clean, simple signature.
- **Give the prospect context.**
- Send **real messages** — it'll take you **10–15 minutes** each.
- **No fluff — just say the thing.**

### 🟢 CONTINUE — other tips
- **Play the long game.**
- **Plant seeds forever.**
- Expect **6–12 months for BIG results.**
- Don't be corny.
- **Pitch people the idea of helping *their* clients more than promoting your own agency.**
- **Add a profile picture in Gmail.**

> **Learning from real inboxes:** the materials include a swipe file of outreach emails actually received (good and bad). Recurring lessons: a creative hook earns the open (the "Messi shirt" / "missing socks" openers), but it must pivot fast to *their* specific problem and real numbers; "guarantees," "DFY," "get X in Y days," and one-size templates read as spam; the strongest ones (e.g. the "soft selling" reply, the ops-founder "89 employees" opener) lead with a sharp, specific observation and a genuine question.

## 6. The operational layer — running it with VAs / AI agents

This is the engine room. **Every step here is executable by a VA or an AI agent.**

### Role of the VA / AI agent — the daily workflow

```
 Source & Qualify ──→ If OK, add lead ──→ Send outreach ──→ Move to
 (30–50/day)          to Pipedrive       manually based     "outreached"
                      (tracker)          on template        in Pipedrive
                                         framework
```

Supporting detail:
- **Sourcing toolkit:** use tools · social media · get email · founder profiles · join platforms · LinkedIn.
- **Volume:** 30–50 sourced/day; **10–20 emails/day minimum.**
- **Tracking:** log every qualified lead in **Pipedrive** (the CRM/tracker); update **daily.**
- **Follow-up scheduling:** Source → Outreach → **Day 2 / Day 5 / Day 10** follow-ups, all tracked.

The VA/agent's three jobs in one line: **writing outreach · generating leads · qualification.**

> **AI-agent translation:** an agent should source 30–50 qualifying leads/day via scraping + platform APIs + social lookups, run both qualification layers automatically, enrich (founder, email, socials, traffic), draft a Section-1/2/3 personalised email from the template framework, write it to Pipedrive, send 10–20/day, and auto-queue Day 2/5/10 follow-ups with rotated angles — escalating only genuine replies to a human (or a closer agent).

### The Lead Generation Executive role (what "good" looks like)

The human/agent profile this system hires for:
- **Mission:** source prospective creators/clients who meet the criteria, build the relationship, and **secure a meeting for the sales team to close.**
- **Does:** search & identify prospects; review their channels/content/analytics against criteria; contact and pitch relevant services; follow up to entice a meeting; coordinate meetings with the sales team; assist deal admin; **keep the CRM current daily; report daily** on leads, responses, and meetings.
- **Is:** interested in the niche/creator economy; sales-minded; confident managing relationships prospect→outreach; clear written English; can learn a CRM; a self-starter driven by KPIs.
- **Sample task (the audition):** *(1) name 3 creators you think are great and why; (2) write an outreach email to a specific creator explaining why they're successful and why they should launch [product] with you.* This doubles as the AI-agent eval — can it identify good targets and write the value-first email?

### End-of-Day (EOD) reporting

The acquisition team (VA or agent) reports daily. EOD email template:

> **MAIN ACQUISITION** — Sourced: _ · Outreach: _ · Responses: _ · Follow-ups: _ · Negotiation: _ · Signed: _
> **Updates** — Pitch needed (who, owner) · Negotiation (who, next step, owner/date)
> **Things that worked well** · **Challenges today**

This makes the pipeline measurable end-to-end and is exactly the kind of structured status an AI agent can emit automatically.

## Synthesis — how this fits the acquisition system

Outbound completes the three-engine acquisition model:

1. **Inbound** (File 1) — content + personal brand pull warm leads to you.
2. **Funnels/VSLs** (File 2) — capture and convert that demand.
3. **Outbound — Hot Outreach** (this file) — proactively source, qualify, and reach the exact clients you want.

Within outbound, the loop is: **Source (30–50/day) → Qualify (2 layers) → Angle → personalised Section 1/2/3 message → log in Pipedrive → Day 2/5/10 follow-ups → hand a booked meeting to the closer.** Then the **deck + two-call sales framework** (*Sign Clients Like Never Before*) closes it, and the offer is priced via *High-Converting Offers*.

**The single biggest idea:** outbound only works when it's *manual-quality at scale*. Strict qualification earns the right to spend 10–15 minutes writing a genuinely researched, value-first, number-backed message that leads with the prospect's problem and helping *their* clients — not your pitch. VAs and AI agents are what make that manual quality repeatable across 10–20 sends a day, every day, for the 6–12 months it takes to compound into big results.
